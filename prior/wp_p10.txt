<p data-p-id="afc8d2866628a19707424a349f5c2bc2">If so, it would be fun if you could just choose the nonces I and M in a way that gives them some meaning.<br>There are two theories in which those two letters as nonces make sense.</p>
<p data-p-id="0495d2d6a45e5d579683a38215d1ba33">The first one is that it just happens that both are in the name IBM.</p>
<p data-p-id="4f74dea7b5b1fb344e74cbe8ee836381">The movie "2001: An Odyssey in Space" calls the antagonist artificial intelligence by the name of Hal, in a play on "IBM". All letters replaced by the letter preceding in the alphabet.</p>
<p data-p-id="41dc81ff504feaf7d93bf78df3b1cc55">So Hal resolves to IBM, with the letters I and M as nonces in the anagram.  What might the third letter B stand for in that case?</p>
<p data-p-id="5f0ef04e11197f8c814d69eff99cd06d">I think "Bitcoin".</p>
<p data-p-id="7badf1842215298fed9026349fcd6502">Sure would be a nice explanation.</p>
<p data-p-id="d4246a9dc424633f84efa522f89ce3c3">The other theory would be that "I M" might be read as "I'm" or "I am".</p>
<p data-p-id="877ed0346f92110f774f296a06fb31ad">That theory resolves THOMAS to "I am Satoshi". Many people know the Voldemort anagram, where the name "Tom Marvolo Riddle" resolves to "I am Lord Voldemort". With another Tom involved here, as it happens.</p>
<p data-p-id="1059885001a44a002aa0f2d116baf4d0">Of course this particular Harry Potter book was published before 2008. </p>
<p data-p-id="bc213234d8f24a871f5fd9bb2ae7be62">That would be exactly the same thing here. Pure genius, if true. Almost as smart as coming up with the Bitcoin network.</p>
<p data-p-id="7ab795caf36fac31b000b182090583f3">I have left out one minor point. Satoshi actually uses the S twice. So how do you explain the extra S in Satoshi? What does it stand for?</p>
<p data-p-id="e7b8bf1b9c9f81ad84ca0eb21ec541ff">And who was it again quoting Lois Lane from Superman with exactly that question on bitcointalk? Might that have been Hal Finney in a post on Bitcointalk discussing the Satoshi identity? If so, wasn't he getting impatient with people still not figuring out this obvious anagram?</p>
<p data-p-id="6e6ef1d3162c95eaa95f91625c695f99">Hal maybe didn't know himself. The most obvious explanation would be "sockpuppet", since that's what Satoshi was for Hal.</p>
<p data-p-id="b637bf3c5fde343d4b068a7fc27ac87e">Another excellent explanation would be that the extra S stands for $, the symbol for the dollar, and by extension, all kind of money. If you create a new currency and have "Satoshi" as its smallest unit, it makes sense to use a sockpuppet name that makes using the dollar sign natural. And the question "what does the S stand for" would have the double meaning of asking why exactly the dollar sign uses the letter S, which is not contained in the word "dollar".</p>
<p data-p-id="02e5254832224b4d134b6ab1a7d0b3e8">And of course, it may just mean "Second". Like in "Harold Thomas Finney II".</p>
<p data-p-id="8fdaaee6ab36cac5f62d2dd01ed4be17">Since clearly Satoshi is an anagram for Thomas, it makes sense to call the basic unit of Grycoin "satommi", honoring Thomas for eternity by his real name as opposed to only by his sockpuppet name.</p>
<p data-p-id="22a2e2b672e53eb42771dca94a8a8ebc"><b>c) BITcoin is actually AHScoin</b> </p>
<p data-p-id="155d5f55ac7eb797568e6e3e0ac8b3cc">There is another interesting coincidence. Why is Bitcoin called Bitcoin and not Intercoin or some other possible choice? Might there be a hidden meaning in this as well, just like with Satoshi's birthday and the Thomas anagram?</p>
<p data-p-id="3d7723f16c58f30e37f3a0be03a51897">The letters preceding "BIT" in the alphabet are "AH" and "S".</p>
<p data-p-id="bb92640330d3fa410b06482dd9dd87f4">Again, we have a partial match. Two of those letters are in "Hal" as well. I leave it as an exercise for the reader to work out the chances of that happening by coincidence. Leaves the "S", which would be another nonce.</p>
<p data-p-id="f5ed75710c2379e15c6e74df076a401b">And obviously HAL has often been speculated to be a code for IBM in the science fiction novel and film "2001: A Space Odyssey" , so playing around with that a bit would be something coming naturally.<br>And again, who was it on record asking "what does the S stand for"?</p>
<p data-p-id="ab10092838d19be565d7f5e818bce994">So, we have an imperfect anagram of HAL, with exactly one letter as a nonce, just like Satoshi has exactly one imperfection.</p>
