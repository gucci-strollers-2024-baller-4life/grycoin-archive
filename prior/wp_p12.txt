<p data-p-id="505d4c507388a06aba985ee8074073d0">When Satoshi announced the first release of the software, I grabbed it right away. I think I was the first person besides Satoshi to run bitcoin. I mined block 70-something, and I was the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction, when Satoshi sent ten coins to me as a test. I carried on an email conversation with Satoshi over the next few days, mostly me reporting bugs and him fixing them."</p>
<p data-p-id="5d484c2ea8e028126560f9df4000c001">We are directed to look at the last letter of each of these. Because we are directed by the fact that the "recognize the signs" instruction comes last in the paragraph.</p>
<p data-p-id="0da738cdc87bcb0f1a6a23ff9f89af8b">STNM</p>
<p data-p-id="8d54b7673a50fc3f26abdceb57fbc326">I wonder what that could mean. Combined with the I as the first letter in the paragraphs close to that. The paragraph containing the words "I made an attempt to make my own proof-of-work based currency, called Bitcoin."</p>
<p data-p-id="54f4aeb577f65e66a9a7aee887e27445">I STNM.</p>
<p data-p-id="5b75bcb33116dcafec61cf59ea5259f3">That's a fun coincidence. And it dovetails nicely with "Nm ST" as a way used to sign in the genesis block. Exactly the same signature.</p>
<p data-p-id="4e18b5a42a924c28cdccfc91fe2b406f">And by the way, the FF and WW has meaning as well, as a dedication message. WW obviously is UUUU or "four U, for you". I leave it as an exercise for the reader to figure out who FF is.</p>
<p data-p-id="fb4e390b87262dc0dae6bdb9044d08f4">The first four and last four words of the paragraph hinting at the solution.</p>
<p data-p-id="fe288f1504837b3bf0d58185e1d9b059">"Today, Satoshi's real identity"</p>
<p data-p-id="14e4687a2c9d9bf9585cbc9ed5cbc0ac">"I recognize the signs."</p>
<p data-p-id="882f0742eadf05aff3204c8f01c79659">Take the first words of that together and you get "Today, I". Like, "today I come out as Satoshi".</p>
<p data-p-id="604527314cea290cdd2677eb49142257">Take the next three words together and you get "Satoshi's real identity, recognize the signs". Like, "if you want to know Satoshi's real identity, recognize the signs (at the end of the relevant paragraphs).</p>
<p data-p-id="435fc9ff2622b3c9e8de91caaf1c8e49"><b>3. Other evidence</b></p>
<p data-p-id="9ec2231d0cb017e794e2f8688e068391">Even if you agree that Hal Finney outed himself as Satoshi in the coded form explained above, that doesn't mean much without other evidence pointing to him. There is a certain famous fake Satoshi around. He does not deserve to have his name mentioned here. If he had written this outing post, people would just laugh some more about him.</p>
<p data-p-id="350600afa6f31170b56cf937b302f42f">But there are multiple strong reasons pointing to Hal Finney in the first place.</p>
<p data-p-id="d911e17d91c236fb8fb6193e9bb262e2"><b>a) ALS Condition</b></p>
<p data-p-id="c9e9e65e736c9e36cb5cfe44785bdad1">Hal Finney was diagnosed with the terminal disease ALS just about the same time Satoshi vanished. I am not aware of any other good reason that Satoshi retired from the project at that particular time.</p>
<p data-p-id="ccd8d4933f7924c4b320f4041758288f"><b>b) Previous Experience</b></p>
<p data-p-id="cb7c94638497d9e55318b3bd51f14e7e">Hal Finney has shown with the PGP project that he was able to turn a good idea into a working software project. He has shown with his running a remailer that he cared deeply about the main reason for developing Bitcoin, which is helping privacy with code.</p>
<p data-p-id="855c70e734ddd0c37a8beab9c67e7230"><b>c) Mail Conversation With Himself</b></p>
<p data-p-id="63d202b15c4c39cece8c4a17dba464d8">Hal Finney had expert knowledge about the problem of concealing an identity. When denying being Satoshi later on, he produced an e-mail conversation with his sockpuppet Satoshi. He would be expected to do exactly that. Having experience with government censure of PGP, he would not want to be exposed to similar criminal prosecution for the Bitcoin project. And having been on record predicting $10 million per bitcoin in January 2009, he was certainly aware of the potential of drawing the unwanted attention of criminals to his wealth early on.</p>
<p data-p-id="e5d4141e4083bb4dc78a963711f9b3b1"><b>d) Not Citing RPOW in the Whitepaper</b></p>
<p data-p-id="162b08f57287bc176d407346142ed60c">Anyone smart enough to come up with Bitcoin certainly would have heard about RPOW  in 2008. Not citing it in the whitepaper is an obvious clue that he wanted to avoid calling attention to his real identity.</p>
<p data-p-id="d7324c205213c995ad2d6a23c5226ac2"><b>e) Coding Style</b></p>
<p data-p-id="da020ca3b5d70015b1152663a9f9f07a">When denying being Satoshi, Hal Finney said that he was more familiar with coding in C, as opposed to the coding style used by Satoshi. Doesn't add up. Guess what language is used in the coding section of the whitepaper? Yes. It was C.</p>
<p data-p-id="9bbe754270f9390198de71148bb9ff56"><b>f) Overheating Computer</b></p>
<p data-p-id="436a7e92bf7d2bc03f58406fd15d83f0">Hal Finney is on record for having exactly the same opportunity to mine early bitcoins as his sockpuppet Satoshi. His story later is that he stopped mining after a while because he was worried about his computer overheating.</p>
<p data-p-id="e664f9fe4b2bb77993d3fd4a10a4f7fc">Sure. A lifelong dream to create e-cash. $10 million per coin predicted. But worried about a computer overheating.</p>
<p data-p-id="cb3dfccb7f1646eedd99d0273f3dabb9">If there really was such a problem at the time, obviously Hal Finney would have been able to solve it in less than five minutes fully drunk and half asleep. Or Satoshi could have fixed it for him. Hal said he reported bugs to Satoshi and Satoshi fixed them. Why not fix the overheating problem then? Because no such problem existed in the first place.</p>
<p data-p-id="da2891ad6b4b93c542e58d868e5ab1b3">If that part is clearly a false statement, one wonders what else he might have tried to hide?</p>
<p data-p-id="0178a486b81672063b35d92196ad077e"><b>4. What Does it Mean?</b></p>
<p data-p-id="b870fa3c6ab235b590175f56489af8b2">If we agree that Hal Finney was Satoshi, there are a couple of things that follow.</p>
<p data-p-id="6f91df7433924c417edffe4dcdd3835b">As long as there is no second coming back from the cryo-conservation (as described above) Satoshi is dead. </p>
<p data-p-id="d6fb83cb0b96376958b5edff0fe09d8c">The torch now is with the rest of humanity. With everyone who wants to assist achieving the whole potential of the project. Such potential including now a solution to the climate crisis.</p>
<p data-p-id="ffb197c8baa52fa55aa051ade8de68fb">The image of Satoshi as a young and healthy man will be replaced by one of an older guy fighting a very unpleasant terminal disease. Maybe Hal did not want that to happen, since it might be unhelpful for Bitcoin's success. I disagree. Actually carrying on with the Bitcoin project even under his circumstances is a great match for Bitcoin the honey badger. Bad-ass crypto old guard fighting almost to the last breath. Should be inspiration for the rest of us to try harder.</p>
<p data-p-id="04ed4b254fb1dad36174aa6f38f58a81">And while Hal Finney may have been too modest to actually claim the fame that should be his for this invention, the rest of us should be free to remember him along with his legacy.</p>
<p data-p-id="535b820117ace74deb4f372652b6813d">As he writes in this post: "But I came by my bitcoins through luck, with little credit to me." That's Satoshi talking about deserving little credit for Bitcoin. While at first glance this is just rather amusing self-deprecating irony, he's actually right. There was little credit to Hal Finney until now.</p>
<p data-p-id="958abd47506c809be80bf4c27bd15011">I for one think that should change.</p>
<p data-p-id="36487286d7442764da7b5f958be4b955">At least for those of us who are able to recognize the signs.</p>
