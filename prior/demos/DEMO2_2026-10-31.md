# Demo (grycoin block 2): structure, rules, and the first open slice of the space

Anchor set for this file: question md5 `7759227d7406d8230d7e3a8f7b9846d7` (author-confirmed in
`as_cleczc`), solution oracle prefix `3c6`, escrow `1tzieUfbeQghz2zjDeGHcAEfzCRgX6eLi`
(unreproduced; solver puzzleponky, 2019-08-03).

## 1. Structure (verified)

1,549 bytes, 10 paragraphs, 15 sentences, 1,197 letters, **25 capitals**.

| # | offset | initial | first-sentence last word |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | I | that |
| 2 | 96 | A | capitalization |
| 3 | 258 | F | capitalizatioN |
| 4 | 419 | F | signs |
| 5 | 535 | I | himself |
| 6 | 721 | O | i |
| 7 | 925 | W | himselF |
| 8 | 1009 | W | signs |
| 9 | 1150 | I | block |
| 10 | 1392 | A | mind |

**Every paragraph ends with a period.** The stage-one rule ("flip the paragraph's last letter") is
physically inapplicable in this text, which is what the demo exists to teach. The replacement unit
is the final letter of the last word of the **first sentence**.

Verified against her own worked example: byte **535** is paragraph 5's initial `I` (lowercase it) and
byte **652** is the `f` of "himself", the last letter of the last word of paragraph 5's first
sentence (uppercase it). Both match the text of paragraphs 6-8 exactly.

So the demonstrated signature rule is: **paragraph initial down, first sentence's final word-letter
up.** Applied to all ten paragraphs that is 18 flips; restricted to initials in `{I,F,W}` (she calls
paragraph 5's `I` one of the signs, and `FFWW` is stage one's marker) that is 12 flips.

## 2. Rule battery: negative

`demos/rules_demo.py`. 17 deterministic rule families (signature rule over all paragraphs, over
`FWI`, over `FW`, over `I` alone; all capitals down; sentence-initial down plus sentence-final up;
every word-final up; the four `signs` words; the stated pair; and each of those plus the stated
pair), both serialisations, 54 candidates. **0 hits on the `3c6` oracle, 0 on the escrow.**

## 3. First genuinely open slice of the space, swept: negative

Closed before this run: all 2^33 boundary subsets, every paragraph/sentence-unit subset, word-edge
k<=3, up-to-4-anywhere, 5-at-word-edges.

`demos/demo_open_space.py` covers two families outside all of those:

- **A** every subset of the 25 capitals, Gray-code walk toggling one byte per step (2^25). Sizes
  <=4 are inside "4-anywhere", size 5 inside "5-boundary"; sizes 6..25 were open.
- **B** four capitals plus one letter anywhere: C(25,4) x 1,172.

**48,380,232 candidates, 200 s at ~240k md5/s. 11,815 hit the 3c6 prefix (expected 11,810 for a
random text), every one derived at indices 0-2: zero escrow matches.**

## 4. The wall, measured

md5 over these 1,549 bytes runs 240-384k/s here.

| space | size | time at 240k/s |
|---|---|---|
| 4 arbitrary flips | 8.5e10 | 4 days |
| 5 arbitrary flips | 2.0e13 | 2.7 years |
| 6 arbitrary flips | 4.0e15 | 530 years |

The frontier is exactly five arbitrary flips, which is also why the GPU hunt's >100B candidates
covers "up to four everywhere" and stops there.

## 5. One constraint worth keeping

The demo's certified question hash is the **LF** serialisation: only `\n\n` joins reproduce
`7759227...`; `\r\n\r\n` gives `e9970079...`. She says not to change anything with the line breaks,
so the `3c6` solution hash must also be LF. Any future demo run can drop the join dimension, a 4x
saving. (Stage one's certificate, `19TbyN5...`, is a separate anchor and is CRLF/cp1252.)
