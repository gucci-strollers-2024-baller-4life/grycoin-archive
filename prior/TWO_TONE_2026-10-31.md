# The dots image has two ink tones, split per glyph

**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Script:** `sweeps/two_tone.py`
**Layer comparison:** `assets/dots_image_layers.png` (full, gray layer, black layer, stacked)

The correction I owe from `DOT_IMAGE_2026-10-31.md`: I said the strip was solid single-colour text.
That was wrong. It is drawn in **two intensities**, and the split is per character.

## The measurement

Strokes do not vary within a glyph. A vertical stem sampled down 30 rows reads 195, 114, 111, 112,
112, 167 at fixed x, and a horizontal crossbar sampled across 25 columns reads 108, 98, 113, 103,
110, 112 … 122, 113, 105, 118, 113, 107 — flat. But the level differs by glyph:

| tone | stroke core | count |
|---|---|---|
| black | 40–55 | 19 glyphs |
| mid-gray | ~110 | 16 glyphs |

Per-glyph classification is cleanly bimodal: every glyph scores either 0.00 or above 0.83 on
dark-fraction, nothing in between, so the two-tone split is real and not JPEG noise.

## The pattern

```
1A1zP1eP5QGefi2DMPTfTL5SLmv7DivfNa.
00011111111000001011100101011110100
```

black glyphs: `zP1eP5QG` `M` `TfT` `S` `m` `7Div` `N`
gray glyphs: `1A1` `efi2DM` `P` `L5` `v` `f` `a.`

Read as two streams, each layer is a fragment of the same address, not a second string. OCR of the
black layer alone returns `zP1eP5QG`, `P`, `fTL`, `L`, `Divf`, `a`; OCR of the gray layer alone
returns `1A1`, `efi2DM`, `5S.m73`. Both are the address, interleaved.

## Decodings tried, all negative

| scheme | result |
|---|---|
| 5-bit groups, offset 0, both polarities | `D?QLSXU` / `?APUNIL` |
| 5-bit groups, offset 1 | `H?AXFP` / `YA?I?Q` |
| 7-bit ASCII, offsets 0 and 1 | `\x0fx\x17\x15t` / `\x1fp.+` |
| Morse by run length | `3 8 5 1 1 3 2 1 1 1 1 4 1 1 2`, no valid grouping |
| Atbash of either stream | no legible string |

35 glyphs is 7 × 5, which is why Bacon was the first thing to try, and it fails.

## Why the bitstream may still be wrong

Five of the 26 raw blocks are merged glyph pairs (`fi`, `Tf`, `Lm`, `ivfN` and the final `a.`), so
the 35-way split is forced at minimum-ink columns and single-glyph boundaries carry ±1 uncertainty.
The two tones are only ~65 levels apart in a JPEG, so a thin stroke on a light background can land
either side of the threshold.

The fix is a lossless copy. X stores the upload as 1191 × 99 JPEG with no alt text and one media
item only; there is no larger variant. A PNG of the same strip, or a screenshot at 200% or more,
would let the segmentation be exact.
