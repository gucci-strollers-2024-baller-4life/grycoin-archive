## The derivation engine, and a calibration bug worth recording

`fastaddr.py` reimplements the chain with `coincurve` + `hashlib` only: 23.2 ms → **1.63 ms** per
derivation, a 14× speedup that made the 33,010-candidate derivation a one-minute job instead of thirteen.

It failed its first calibration and the failure was instructive: the hardened levels of the BIP32 chain were
computed as `IL mod N` instead of `(IL + k_par) mod N`. Stage one caught it instantly, because stage one is a
solved block and a wrong chain cannot accidentally reproduce `19TbyN5…`. **This is the practical value of the
calibrated exemplar: it turns a silent derivation bug into a loud one.**

# Addendum: the transport model, run and closed

**The claim to test.** That the answer is a set of *coupled transports* (a capital leaving a unit head and
landing on a unit tail), and that an engine enumerating independent single-character flips cannot find it.

**The correction.** It can: the sweeper flips both directions within every candidate, so a transport is two
flips whose directions are constrained, which is a *subset* of the space already enumerated, not a different
machine. What the transport framing correctly identifies is that the certified stage-one answer is an
**eight-edit shape** (four head-downs, four tail-ups), and no sweep before this one had reached eight edits.
That gap was real, and the constraint is what makes it reachable.

**The enumeration.** For the demo: heads = the 10 paragraph initials; tails = either the 10 paragraph finals
or the 10 first-sentence finals; every pair of sets (D, U) with |D| = |U|, which is exactly the condition that
a bijective transport exists; direction semantics enforced (a down-move requires a capital, an up-move
requires a lower-case letter); four serializations; k from 1 to 10.

Far below the theoretical C(20,10) = 184,756 per pass, because direction semantics bite hard: paragraph 3's
final letter is already `N` and paragraph 7's is already `F` (the author's own quoted examples), so only eight
of the ten paragraphs can receive an up-move at all, and equal-count pairing narrows it further.

So the demo is not a transport over its paragraph boundaries, at any size, in either tail definition, in any
serialization. The eight-edit shape is reachable and negative.

**What the transport model does not cover.** Units at *sentence* granularity: 15 heads, 15 tails, balanced,
C(30,15) = 155,117,520 configurations, about five minutes of the same engine. That is the only remaining
form of the hypothesis.
