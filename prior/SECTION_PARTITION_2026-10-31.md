# The section partition: three sections yield four anomalies, only one carries the signature

**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Script:** `sweeps/section_partition.py` (results in `section_partition_results.json`)
**Derivations:** 180 across the five four-anomaly sections, two alphabets. Nothing here interacts
with any earlier negative.

## 1. Method

Sections are the chapter's 18 structural headings: the three acts, their numbered sections, and the
`a)`–`f)` subheadings. Each heading opens a section running to the paragraph before the next
heading. **Headings are excluded from their own section**, so a title cannot be counted as one of
its anomalies. That exclusion matters: without it "4. Why Blockchain?" looks like a four-anomaly
section purely because its title opens on `W`.

Both alphabets are run, since `fb3cb47` established `{A,I,M,O,S,T}` as the minimal set giving
exactly four anomalies on both the Finney post and the grycoin demo, while several notes quote the
92-paragraph pool that belongs to the seven-letter form:

| alphabet | carriers | anomalies |
|---|---|---|
| `AIMOST` | 169 | 103 |
| `AIMOSTH` | 180 | 92 |

Every section with exactly four prose anomalies is then derived under the certified transport,
four serialisations, indices 0/1/2/44/76/77.

## 3. Why cardinality alone is not a filter

Four of the 28 detectable partitions land on exactly four: three sections plus the section that
only appears to because of its own heading. At section sizes of 4 to 27 paragraphs, exactly-four is
what chance supplies, and most of the small sections cannot reach four at all. What separates W3
from `GFVE` and `WVWB` is not the count, it is the signature.

So the section partition confirms the known window and supplies no candidate. Under the ¶241
reading, where the chapter's marks need not spell `FFWW` at all, cardinality plus section
boundaries is too weak on its own; a second constraint is required, and the only one in the text is
the dedication that names which four-letter message to expect.

## 4. Combined with the sign census

The two audits answer each other. The sign census shows the vocabulary is concentrated in Acts III
and IV and absent from Act II; the section partition shows the four-anomaly coincidences cluster in
the middle acts and that the only signature-bearing one is W3. Together they close the per-act
route from both ends: the semantic channel has no Act II member to give, and the orthographic
channel has no signature-eligible Act II member to give either.
