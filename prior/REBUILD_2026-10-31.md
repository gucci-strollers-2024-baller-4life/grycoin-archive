## 2. Correction: there are seventeen authored trailing spaces, not thirteen

Every paragraph's stored `data-p-id` is the md5 of its text. That lets the source be audited
paragraph by paragraph: 272 of 273 paragraphs verify under `md5(tags stripped)`.

The trailing-space census in the corpus lists thirteen paragraphs (86, 108, 122, 128, 139, 147, 165,
166, 192, 201, 209, 220, 267). The live source has **seventeen**: the same thirteen, plus four that
carry a space immediately *before* an internal line break rather than at the paragraph end:

    63, 121, 124, 160

So the authored trailing spaces split 13 paragraph-final + 4 pre-break. The base is unaffected,
because it preserves text verbatim and both certified digests reproduce; but the census was
incomplete, and any reading that normalises whitespace now has 17 positions to be careful about, not
13. All four of the new ones sit inside the internal-`<br>` set (63, 73, 121, 124, 146, 160, 184,
189, 193, 213).

## 3. One paragraph fails its own integrity hash

Paragraph 78 is `"What?"` — the second of the two identical short lines (the first is 34, forty-four
paragraphs earlier). Its stored `data-p-id` matches its content under **no** transform tried: raw,
rstripped, tags stripped, tags stripped and rstripped, `<br>` removed, `<br>` as newline.

Every other paragraph matches under at least one, and 272 of 273 match under the plain
`tags stripped` rule. So paragraph 78 is the single place in the chapter where the stored id and the
stored text disagree — the signature of a paragraph edited after it was first saved.

This matters only as provenance: the certified base is built from the text, not the id, and the text
is what the 273-paragraph rebuild reproduces. But it is the only paragraph in the chapter whose
publication history is not self-consistent, and it is one of the two duplicate lines, which the
corpus has already flagged as suspicious.
