<p data-p-id="8caf0b64d95bdbadb43b802733b44d0e">Hal is on record saying "I am comfortable with my legacy" as the last sentence of a post titled "Bitcoin and me" with the thin disguise that he was talking about an inheritance of a small number of bitcoins, as opposed to the legacy of creating the Bitcoin network.</p>
<p data-p-id="0ab1a46c3f39e45a6e305894199578ec">In that post he wrote "I had long been interested in cryptographic payment schemes. Plus I was lucky enough to meet and extensively correspond both with Wei Dai and Nick Szabo, generally acknowledged to have created ideas that would be realized with Bitcoin. I had made an attempt to make my own proof-of-work based currency, called RPOW. So I found Bitcoin..."</p>
<p data-p-id="67071ceabe2e7562af6fd4a03390d419">That would be exactly what one would expect Satoshi himself saying in a post outing himself. And the title of that post would also be exactly what one would expect.</p>
<p data-p-id="66ce0953c13493631b6e3ecf97639eeb">And that is actually very close to outing himself. Just remove a couple of words and you get "I made an attempt to make my own proof-of-work based currency, called Bitcoin."</p>
<p data-p-id="102f84dc3aac43c5a51e1b1c076d382f">Actually, that particular post has some other interesting points to note. Once you know about the anagram above, you will expect it to have some hidden meaning. And it will become very easy to recognize the signs. </p>
<p data-p-id="fdbff32e05bcbb12f3a531fb8eb1bef1">Hal writes this:</p>
<p data-p-id="d6df7b89db018ad9c7c5f23bc395a6b9">"Today, Satoshi's true identity has become a mystery. But at the time, I thought I was dealing with a young man of Japanese ancestry who was very smart and sincere. I've had the good fortune to know many brilliant people over the course of my life, so I recognize the signs."</p>
<p data-p-id="a9b1d610a859490df82b0ff71e9810e3">There is the somewhat strange point of saying "recognize the signs." And saying that when discussing Satoshi's identity.</p>
<p data-p-id="cc559bf8528e38126900c52bab66141a">One way to understand this is as "I have met very smart people before, so I recognize when I see one." But why say that when you are talking about identity, not about intelligence? Seems odd.</p>
<p data-p-id="f5478339a792c30b7f5967f43d51ae70">So what's up with that "I recognize the signs" part?</p>
<p data-p-id="2b3ccd9b6c211bf9aad5db9c5481df30">Maybe that should be read as the reader should try to "recognize the signs" to decode the hidden meaning, as opposed to where to look for signs of intelligence?</p>
<p data-p-id="a11f428669e007c2adc79c9e1f2133a5">That reading would mean look at "the signs seen before."</p>
<p data-p-id="8dbd18ad4812eb48d170fb4b314c04a2">Turns out, there are some signs we recognize from the Satoshi anagram. The letters I, T, A, S, and M as first letters of each paragraph of this post.</p>
<p data-p-id="11e9ee6c707b3ed78c90795a53f17816">All paragraphs except for four, which start with F F and W W.</p>
<p data-p-id="e436d1de0b5f2b2b6f1ec715cdf0a207">Having seen the signs before, we are directed to look at those four paragraphs, the only ones where we don't recognize the signs. Those paragraphs are (with one paragraph starting with I in between):</p>
<p data-p-id="2046bd8b5032c05d5637832fba69df56">"For those who don't know me, I'm Hal Finney. I got my start in crypto working on an early version of PGP, working closely with Phil Zimmermann. When Phil decided to start PGP Corporation, I was one of the first hires. I would work on PGP until my retirement. At the same time, I got involved with the Cypherpunks. I ran the first cryptographically based anonymous remailer, among other activities.</p>
<p data-p-id="a5ca0005cb2d75bf62c872b3ac6aedde">Fast forward to late 2008 and the announcement of Bitcoin. I've noticed that cryptographic graybeards (I was in my mid 50's) tend to get cynical. I was more idealistic; I have always loved crypto, the mystery and the paradox of it.</p>
<p data-p-id="87f67650ba29639bd482dc8d06d6dadf">When Satoshi announced Bitcoin on the cryptography mailing list, he got a skeptical reception at best. Cryptographers have seen too many grand schemes by clueless noobs. They tend to have a knee jerk reaction.</p>
<p data-p-id="2a16a8ce6de2375c9fcf5ae89444c6c7">I was more positive. I had long been interested in cryptographic payment schemes. Plus I was lucky enough to meet and extensively correspond with both Wei Dai and Nick Szabo, generally acknowledged to have created ideas that would be realized with Bitcoin. I had made an attempt to create my own proof of work based currency, called RPOW. So I found Bitcoin fascinating.</p>
